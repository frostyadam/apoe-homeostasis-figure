No E3/E3 referenceEvery contrast compares E4 carriers with E2 carriers. A shift can come from either allele.
The LXR target evidence is not brain evidenceOf the eight genes lower in APOE4 carriers, five are direct LXR targets, but the mapping was done elsewhere. APOE, APOC1 and APOC2 respond through the ME.1 and ME.2 multienhancers, which were defined as macrophage- and adipose-specific enhancers, and CD36's element is a mouse liver DR-7. No LXR response element has been mapped in brain; astrocyte and in-vivo brain APOE do respond to LXR agonists, but the element is unknown [12]. LRP1 rests on one protein-level study, and CLU/APOJ and LIPA have no credible evidence at all. LIPA in fact sits upstream: it generates the oxysterol ligands that activate LXR.
CE is E2's shift, not E4'sThe one dataset with all three allele arms (3545 people, plasma) finds polyunsaturated CE depleted in E2 (CE 22:6 FDR 3.6×10⁻⁵) and nothing at all in E4 (0 of 553 at FDR < 0.10) — so a CE difference in an E4-vs-E2 contrast is attributable to E2 [8]. Plasma is not brain, so the isogenic and ROSMAP CE results still stand.
BMP unconfirmed in humansEvery BMP number here is mouse or cell-line. The only APOE-stratified human data (89 AD brains, five genotypes) trend the opposite way, and di-22:6-BMP has never been measured in any APOE-stratified sample [9]. Treat it as a failed prediction, not an established human APOE4 feature.
Genotype tracks ancestryAbout 59% of LC E4 carriers are of African ancestry, against about 33% of E2 carriers. Group differences cannot be attributed to genotype alone. Panel D's EA/AA split is the check for receptors.
Density, not stateMicroglial signal in Visium is compartment size, not per-cell change: Xenium per-cell expression is flat. It is also African-ancestry-specific (AA −0.91 vs EA −0.12). Read glial rows as abundance.
cis windowGenes within 1 Mb of APOE (‡) are dropped from every step score. The cis window is enriched for DE (29.4% vs 11.7% background), which fits linkage rather than pathway biology.

Not established: CLU/APOJ and LIPA. The PTGDS assignment is withdrawn: Fujimori et al. (JBC 2007;282:18458) did map two SREs at −194 with SREBP-1c binding by EMSA, ChIP and siRNA, but in murine 3T3-L1 adipocytes, and the mechanism has never been replicated in any other cell type. Decisively, the same group dissected the human L-PGDS promoter from −730 to +75 in human brain-derived TE671 cells — a window that contains −194 — and found activity attributable to Hes-1 at −337 and AP-2β at −98, with no functional SRE (Fujimori, Kadoyama & Urade, JBC 2005, PMID 15743775). “Indirect LXR target via SREBP-1c” is therefore not a defensible label for PTGDS in human brain. PTGDS was itself removed from this figure (2026-09-29) and replaced by APOD. PTGDS passed every artefact and composition check — its standard error is the 3rd percentile of all genes, it is not restricted to any compartment, it is ancestry-invariant, and it is anti-correlated with a leptomeningeal module (r = −0.74) — so the decrease is real. It was cut because it has no lipoparticle function (no cholesterol binding, no APOE or apoE-receptor interaction in IntAct across 100 records, no ligand-bound structure in 25 PDB entries), no AD genetic association (Open Targets rank 734/13,006, 73 of 74 evidence records text-mining), and because the APOE-genotype evidence that exists has no consistent sign: PTGDS falls in isogenic APOE4/4 iPSC oligodendroglia (Blanchard 2022 Table S12, −1.35) but rises in the same paper's post-mortem oligodendrocytes under a subject-aware mixed model (Table S14, +0.55, padj 0.0096), rises in isogenic mural cells (Schuldt 2025, +0.64, adj.P 7.9e-3), and flips sign between APOE3/3 and APOE3/4 across five entorhinal cell types (Belonwu 2022). Our own decrease matches the in-vitro direction and contradicts the best human post-mortem measurement in the matching cell type. Its PGD2 arm also fails locally: the receptors PTGDR and PTGDR2 are effectively unexpressed in brain parenchyma (ERC snRNA AveExpr −0.07 to −1.82 outside the vasculature), and the AD-associated prostaglandin D synthase is the unrelated gene HPGDS, which is the one well expressed in microglia here (6.59). AGT was removed from this figure (2026-09-28): it is a non-inhibitory serpin whose inhibitory activity was lost in jawed vertebrates (human AGT is a serpin scaffold repurposed as a conformationally regulated prohormone; Carrell & Read Front Cardiovasc Med 2021, PMID 33816576), so its serpin classification affords no mechanistic link to APOE or lipoprotein biology; and the APOE-genotype evidence that does exist does not support a drawn edge. Corrected 2026-09-28: an earlier version of this note claimed that no study reports AGT as a function of APOE genotype. That was false, and it came from a full-text search that cannot see genes appearing only in unindexed supplementary spreadsheets. The corrected position is that AGT is reported in APOE-stratified supplementary DEG tables, but does not survive FDR correction, donor-level modelling or the AD-only stratum, and is null in the one genome-wide test performed in the lineage it actually occupies. Specifically: Sun et al. Nat Neurosci 2023 (PMID 37264161) Supplementary Table 14 lists AGT among APOE3-vs-APOE4 DEGs in fibroblast cluster Fib3 (p 2.2×10−4, coef +0.67) and the authors' own gene set labels it Fib3.E4up — but that table's inclusion rule is p<0.01 rather than FDR-controlled, and AGT's FDR is 0.074, so it does not clear 0.05; the same paper's Table 16 does place AGT as an APOE4-specific pericyte cognitive-decline gene (Per1.APOE4, FDR 0.044, absent from Per1.APOE3). Blanchard et al. Nature 2022 (PMID 36385529) Table S12 reports AGT up 0.671 → 12.185 FPKM (log2FC +4.18) in isogenic APOE3/3-vs-APOE4/4 iPSC oligodendroglia, ranking 129 of 15,100 by test statistic — but its q value is the cuffdiff permutation floor shared by 4,516 genes, the rise is off a near-zero baseline, and by that paper's own annotation (Table S2) AGT is expressed in astrocytes, pericytes and smooth muscle and not in oligodendrocytes, while the APOE4/4 culture gained astrocytic and mural identity wholesale (GFAP +43×, AQP4 +125×, ALDH1L1 +54×, DCN +38×, LUM +32×, ACTA2 +14×). The rise therefore tracks the contaminating lineage AGT belongs to. Both of that paper's donor-level APOE4 models are null — NEBULA −0.186, padj 0.64, sign opposite; pseudo-bulk limma adj.P 1.00 — the AD-only stratum loses significance and flips sign (padj 0.76), and a donor-level re-test of its per-individual averages is null in every cell type (min BH q 0.71). Most directly, Schuldt et al. (bioRxiv 2025, PMID 40964350) run a genome-wide isogenic APOE4/4-vs-APOE3/3 comparison in mural cells, the lineage where AGT is genuinely expressed, and AGT is null (−0.226, adj.P 0.988); it is likewise null in APOE4/4-vs-APOE3/3 iPSC microglia (Haney et al. Nature 2024, PMID 38480892, padj 0.92). The astrocyte case specifically is untested rather than negative: TCW et al. Cell 2022 published no gene-level DEG table, and AGT is robustly expressed in their public isogenic featureCounts (GSE190185), so that question remains open. The earlier “indirect LXR via SREBP-1c” assignment was wrong on two counts: no sterol regulatory element has been mapped in the AGT promoter, and the published AGT–SREBP-1c relationship runs the other way (hepatocyte Agt deletion suppresses SREBP1, Tao et al. J Lipid Res 2019, PMID 31604805). AGT is oxysterol-inducible in neurons and astrocytes in an LXR-dependent manner (Mateos et al. JBC 2011;286:25574, sign inconsistent between endogenous oxysterols and T0901317), which is a testable hypothesis rather than a target assignment. AGT is a member of the QIAGEN/IPA “LXR/RXR Activation” canonical pathway and PTGDS is not, which is the likely provenance of the error. TRRUST, the only purely literature-curated TF–target resource, returns none of the eight for human LXR; database hits for the remaining three resolve to peak proximity, motif-only matches, co-expression or IPA pathway membership.
